Accessible Learning Media

Track 2 — Architecture, capabilities and how people use it

Digital Bridge Accessibility Hackathon 2026 · ALX Tech Hub, Lideta · 14–15 November 2026
Status: proposal for team review (Day one, architecture block). Nothing described here is built yet.

1. Summary

We are building an embeddable video player and a transcript pipeline for learners who are Deaf, hard of hearing, blind, or on a slow connection. It plugs into the platforms learners already use, starting with Savanna, rather than being a separate app.

“I do not need a special app. I need the one everyone uses to work with my phone.” — participant, co-design session, 21 October 2026

3. The player

  • Controls: real <button>, <select> and slider elements, so NVDA and TalkBack announce them correctly. Touch targets at least 44 px. Layout reflows at 200% zoom.
  • Keyboard shortcuts (active only while focus is inside the player): k play/pause, j/l back/forward 10 s, c captions, d descriptions, s sign window, t transcript, f fullscreen.
  • Captions: English and Amharic, drawn by the player so learners can change size, text colour and background. Ethiopic fonts.
  • Sign language window: a second, muted video kept in time with the lesson. Move it by dragging or with arrow keys, resize it with buttons or by dragging, snap it to a corner. Stays visible in fullscreen.
  • Audio description: a text track. Read by the screen reader through a live region, or spoken aloud with the option to pause the lesson while it speaks.
  • Transcript panel: every line is a button that jumps to that moment; the current line is highlighted.
  • Data saver: 240p, 360p or audio only. Defaults to 240p on slow connections. Settings are saved on the learner’s own device.
  • Interface language: English or Amharic.

4. Content model: one source, many outputs

Staff write or review a few small text files per lesson. Every other format is generated from them, so fixing one typo updates everything.

Source fileGenerates
captions.en.vtt, captions.am.vttPlayer captions, transcript HTML, tagged PDF, Braille, offline pack
descriptions.en.vtt (and .am)Player audio description, description lines in every transcript
images.json (alt text for slides and diagrams)Image descriptions in HTML and PDF documents
sign.mp4 (interpreter recording)Sign language window

5. Processing pipeline

  1. Video: ffmpeg makes a 360p MP4, a 240p MP4 (about 200 kbps), an audio-only MP3 (32 kbps mono) and a poster image.
  2. Captions: uploaded by staff, or drafted by local Whisper and marked “needs review”. Nothing is sent to a cloud service.
  3. Documents: tagged HTML transcript; tagged PDF printed by headless Chromium (which keeps document structure); Braille .brf from liblouis.
  4. Offline pack: a zip with the MP3, 240p MP4, caption files and a light index.html.

6. Offline plan for a 3G phone

  • Offline pack (audio, text, light HTML, as the brief asks). Files are named so VLC loads captions automatically. The HTML page embeds the captions inside itself, so it works when opened straight from the phone’s storage.
  • Light streaming: only the poster loads at first; video streams in pieces (HTTP range requests); 240p by default on slow connections.
  • Service worker on our demo page so lessons opened once keep working offline. Optional on host sites, which control their own service worker.

7. API (Level 2)

Described in an OpenAPI 3.1 spec so other platforms can generate client code.

GET  /api/media                    list lessons
POST /api/media                    upload video (admin) → 202 + processing status
GET  /api/media/{id}               manifest: sources, tracks, sign video, documents, JSON-LD
POST /api/media/{id}/tracks        upload a caption or description file (admin)
POST /api/media/{id}/sign          upload sign language video (admin)
GET  /api/media/{id}/transcript    ?lang=en|am &format=html|pdf|brf
GET  /api/media/{id}/offline.zip   offline pack
GET  /embed/{id}                   iframe embed page

GET  /api/jobs                     schema.org/JobPosting list
GET  /api/jobs/{id}/audio          audio summary of a job card
POST /api/badges                   issue an Open Badges 3.0 credential (admin)
GET  /api/badges/{id}              verifiable credential (JSON) and readable page

Security and privacy: uploads and badge issuing need an admin token. Watching lessons is public and needs no account. Other sites may read lesson data (CORS). We store the minimum personal data and nothing leaves the host without consent.

8. How people use it

8.1 Learners

A Deaf learner on Savanna

  • Turns on Amharic captions and makes them larger, yellow on black.
  • The sign language interpreter appears in a corner; they drag it left and enlarge it so it doesn’t cover the slides. It stays in sync when they pause or skip.
  • Rereads anything they missed in the transcript panel and jumps back to that moment.

A blind learner using NVDA or TalkBack

  • Every control is announced, for example “Play, button” or “Captions, English”.
  • With audio description on, the screen reader says “Description: a slide shows the four steps of a CV…” and can pause the lesson while it reads.
  • Downloads the transcript as tagged HTML or PDF with images described, or as Braille (.brf) for a Braille display or embosser.

A learner outside Addis Ababa on a low-cost Android phone with 3G

  • The player starts at 240p; they can switch to audio only.
  • On café Wi-Fi they tap “Download for offline” and get a small zip. At home with no data they open it from their files or in VLC, with captions.

A learner with low vision or a physical disability

  • Everything works at 200% zoom and by keyboard only; buttons are large enough to tap easily.

8.2 Teachers and content staff

  1. Upload a lesson video through the API or a simple admin page.
  2. The server makes 360p, 240p and audio-only versions and a thumbnail.
  3. Local speech recognition optionally drafts English captions; staff correct them and add Amharic captions and audio description text.
  4. Upload the interpreter’s sign language recording.
  5. Transcripts (HTML, PDF, Braille) and the offline pack are generated automatically.

3 · Open standardsAny compatible software, no custom workWebVTT captions work in VLC, Moodle and other players. schema.org VideoObject and JobPosting are readable by catalogues and job search engines. Open Badges 3.0 certificates verify in any badge wallet.

8.4 Graduates and employers (career gateway)

  • A graduate browses accessible job cards (200% zoom, screen reader), with dates in the Ethiopian and Gregorian calendars, and taps Listen for an audio summary in Amharic or English.
  • They share their certificate link with an employer.
  • The employer opens it and sees the course, the issuer and “Verified”. The check uses the server’s digital signature, so a fake or altered certificate fails, with no phone call or login needed.

8.5 What it deliberately does not do

  • Send lesson audio or learner data to outside services.
  • Require an account to watch, or track learners. Settings stay on the learner’s phone.
  • Become a separate app. It plugs into the platforms learners already use.

9. Key decisions

DecisionWhy
Plain web component, not ReactSmallest download on 3G and works on any host site. Helps “can be adopted” and “can be kept running”.
Player draws its own captionsNeeded for resize and recolour; consistent on every browser.
Text-based audio descriptionCheap to write, translatable to Amharic, tiny files. Recorded description audio can be added later.
Files on disk, no databaseNo hosting cost and nothing extra to run.
Speech recognition optional and localNo paid-API lock-in; nothing leaves the host without consent.
One set of caption files as the sourceOne edit updates the player, transcripts, PDF and Braille.

11. Risks and open questions

  • Amharic captions: Whisper’s Amharic is weak. Plan to write or correct them by hand, reviewed by the co-designer or interpreters.
  • Sign language video: we need a real Ethiopian Sign Language recording for the demo. Ask the interpreters in the room for about one minute.
  • Savanna starter: we assume it accepts a script tag. Confirm with the mentor.
  • Time: Braille and the live-session viewer are extras; build them only after the required items pass the gate.
